Making Data-Driven Decisions

Lesson Objectives

  • Complete the Driver Configurations Activity successfully
  • Define the idea of Data and be able to read a basic data table
  • Understand the importance of gathering data and making decisions based on that data

Lesson Overview

The Driver Configurations Activity presents a perfect opportunity to teach students objective decision making in a fairly simple manner. By learning how to record data into and read a data table, students will be equipped with an effective method of gathering and analysing data in future weeks.

Materials Needed

  • Students' Engineering Notebooks
  • Basebots and controllers
  • Data Table Video
  • Driver Configurations Activity

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole group discussion about what “data” is and why it’s potentially important. (5-10 mins)
  • Show the Data Tables video. (5 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts:
    • Who here has ever heard of "data"?
      • What do you think it is?
      • How do you think it is used?
    • Definition of data: When you collect numbers or information that can help you see patterns or make decisions
    • Examples of data usage and collection:
      • Weather: People can use data from the air such as humidity or temperature to predict the weather
      • School Attendance: Teachers can track student’s attendance, and help those who have less
      • sports: people can track which teams score the most pointsduring a season, and see who’s the best

Monitoring Progress

  • Once the video is done, have students go to their Bot groups and create a data table on a fresh page of their Engineering Notebooks (see table details). Then have the students complete the Driver Configurations Activity, recording their data (in seconds) in the appropriate sections of their Data Tables. (30 mins)

Wrapping Up

  • Once all groups are finished collecting their data, have them find the average time for each driving configuration and enter it in their data tables, assisting if needed. If faster groups wish, they can find the average time of each group member as well. Under their data table, have each group write which configurations they pick, explaining why with evidence from the table. (10 mins)

Assessment

  • Successful completion of activity
  • Proper data tables are made within Engineering Notebooks

Preparation for next class

Next class will be the most technically demanding day of the course to this point, so be prepared to answer lots of questions and give lots of help. That being said, also encourage students to try and figure it out on their own, because that’s how they’ll learn the most.
