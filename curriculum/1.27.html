Work Day

Lesson Objectives

  • Document their thoughts and processes in their Engineering Notebooks
  • Test their designs utilizing the Improve your Claw practice activity
  • Optimize their manipulators through testing and iteration

Lesson Overview

This lesson will be the final work day for the manipulator. Because of this, students are going to want to focus on polishing their existing designs rather than complete reinvention, as well as ensuring they have proper documentation for all of their previous work.

Materials Needed

  • Engineering Notebooks
  • VEX IQ Kits
  • Improve your Claw activity
  • Timer

Getting Started

  • Begin with a class discussion about their progress. Ask students to share their designs and the intention behind them. Remind the students that this is the last work day dedicated to the manipulators so they are going to want to ensure their manipulators and Engineering Notebooks are both ready and complete, in order to start the next part of the process in the next lesson. (5 mins)
  • Allow the students to break into their Bot Groups, retrieve their materials, and begin working. While they do, set up the field according to the Improve your Claw activity. (40 mins)

  • Discussion prompts:
    • What final polishing or designing needs to be done on your manipulator?
      • Why is it probably not a good idea to tear your whole design down and start from scratch now?
    • Why did you design your manipulator to do what it does?
    • Have you documented every piece of the process in your Engineering Notebooks?

Monitoring Progress

  • As students are working, circulate around the classroom and check on their build and documentation progress. Make sure to stress that students should be documenting at regular intervals using a variety of methods, and that they should be making sure there are no gaps in their documentation.

  • Documentation Prompts:
    • How do you think you could use numbers to help you show which iterations were and are the best?
    • What kind of modifications did you find the most useful?
    • Are you satisfied with your final design?
    • How can you improve your design?
      • How have you improved it so far?

Wrapping Up

  • Come back together for a whole group discussion about the project. Ask if all students have finished their manipulators and fully documented them in their Notebooks. If someone wants to display their notebook or design, allow them, and ask them to explain them to the class. (5 min)
  • Discussion Prompts:
    • Have you finished everything you needed to finish?
    • Why did you make your design the way you did?
    • How did your earlier documentation help you find your current solution?
    • How did brainstorming and listing out the criteria and constraints of designs helped you build a better design?

Assessment

  • Respectful and lively discussion and collaboration
  • Documentation of every step in Engineering Notebooks
    • Documentation of the whole manipulator design process
    • Adjustments made
    • Observations recorded
    • peer/team contributions

Preparation for next class

The next class introduces the second aspect of the design challenge, and so will make students’ design work more complicated and challenging. The instructor may have to offer moral support and help the students from getting discouraged in order to keep them from feeling like they can’t do the work.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
