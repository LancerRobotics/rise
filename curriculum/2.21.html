Lesson 21: Block Stacking Challenge

Objective(s)

  • Students will learn the rules of the Block Stacking Challenge.
  • Students will start planning their autonomous program with pseudocode and may begin coding.

Materials Needed

  • Notebooks
  • Computers
  • Clawbots

Lesson Procedure

1. Overview

  • In this lesson, students will learn the game rules and begin pseudocoding a strategy for the block stacking competition.

2. Getting Started

  • Introduce the game as an autonomous block stacking competition. Students will use a simple clawbot to stack as many cubes as possible in the goal zone during a timed match.
  • Explain that higher stacks earn more points, single cubes also count for points, and groups may modify parts of the base bot design to fit their strategy.
  • Each group has one minute to run its program.
  • Review scoring: the number of blocks in a stack is multiplied by the stack height. For example, a stack that is 2 cubes high with 3 blocks total earns 6 points.
  • Students must place colored blocks in their matching colored goal, marked with tape. Green blocks go in the green goal and blue blocks go in the blue goal. Any color blocks can go on the line. Only blocks in the goals are scored.

3. Monitoring Progress

  • Make sure students understand the rules, scoring system, time limit, and goal-zone requirements.
  • Check that each group has started thinking about how its clawbot will pick up, move, and stack blocks autonomously.

4. Wrapping Up

  • Students should spend the remaining time discussing different strategies and approaches to winning the game.

Assessment

  • Students understand the rules of the Block Stacking Challenge.
  • Students have an initial strategy idea for the competition.
