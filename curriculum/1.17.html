Making Real World Connections

Lesson Objectives

  • Reflect on students’ progress so far in the course
  • Make connections from classroom learning to the real world
  • Learn about various Engineering careers available to pursue
  • Begin the Real World Solution poster project

Lesson Overview

The purpose of today's lesson is to have students reflect on their experiences in the last lesson, as well as introduce a small project. This project will show students possible careers in Engineering and how it plays a part in their day to day lives.

Materials Needed

  • Students' Engineering Notebooks
  • Pencil and Paper
  • Computer/projector
  • Engineering Careers Reference Sheet

Getting Started

  • Begin with a class discussion about the last lesson. Ask students how they feel after it, what they did and didn’t like, and how they felt making their own creations and then putting them to use. (5-10 mins)
  • After the discussion, have the students take out their Engineering notebooks and a writing utensil, and ask them to reflect on the course so far. Clarify that their reflections are personal, so they are encouraged to cover anything they want in their writing, but there are reflection questions provided to prompt their thinking. Write the Reflection Questions somewhere easily readable for all the students for them to reference as they reflect. (10-15 mins)
  • After the students finish reflecting, pass out paper to each student and ask them to write some brief feedback on the course so far. They are again free to write whatever they please, but ask that they at least answer the Feedback Questions provided. Erase the Reflection Questions from their place and replace them with the Feedback Questions. (5 mins)

  • Discussion prompts:
    • How does it feel to have competed in the Robot Soccer challenge?
    • What was your favorite part of the challenge and why?
    • Did you enjoy getting to work with the robotics kits?
      • Is there any part you wish you had gotten the chance to try or use?
    • Did you like getting to create something using the EDP?

  • Reflection Questions:
    • How did I feel creating something from the ground up using the Engineering Design Process?
      • How does it feel to use this method of thinking? Weird? Comfortable? Somewhere in between, or different entirely?
    • How do I think I have grown over the course of these lessons so far?
    • How did I feel working with my Bot Groupmates?
      • Did I collaborate well? Did it feel good to work in a team, or did it feel like there was too much friction?
    • What would I like to concentrate more on going forward in the course?

  • Feedback Questions:
    • What have you strongly liked or disliked so far in the course?
    • Are there any elements you wish were different, or removed entirely?
    • Are there any elements you would have liked to focus more on?
    • What were your favorite and least favorite activities so far in the course, and why?
    • Do you feel like you have a strong grasp of every concept covered so far in your lessons? Why or why not?

Monitoring Progress

  • After the Reflection and Feedback period, collect and store the feedback papers so they can be referenced in the future when altering the curriculum. Once collected, have a class discussion on how students think engineering plays a role in their daily lives. (5 mins)
  • After the discussion, tell the students that there are many different types of Engineering in the real world, and all those different types of Engineers solve different kinds of problems. For the next couple of lessons, students will be pretending to be real life engineers, and solve real life problems. Open the Engineering Careers Reference Sheet and take the students through it, reading off each type of engineering career as written. Ask the students to save questions for the next phase of the lesson, because they may be answered by other details that they hear later. (10 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts:
    • How many different things do you think you use in your day to day life that were created by an engineer? Why?
    • Do you think that engineering impacts you and your life, or only applies to big things like making robots or skyscrapers?
    • What is one thing you use every day that you want to know how someone invented it? Why?

Wrapping Up

  • After reading through the Engineering Careers Reference Sheet, tell the class that it is time to try and solve a problem they see in their day-to-day lives. Alone or in groups of their choice, no larger than three students, they will engineer a solution to their problem and create a poster for it, clearly showing the problem, their solution, and what kind of engineering their solution falls under. Clarify that they will not be building anything, so they can be as imaginative as they want coming up with their designs, but they should try and follow the EDP when coming up with them. Allow the students to find their groups and get started. Instruct them to make any notes or drawings in a new page of their Engineering Notebook, and advise them to use the steps of the EDP to start coming up with their ideas. (15 mins)
  • At the end of the lesson, tell students they will have the next lesson as time to make their poster and prepare to present it to the rest of the class.

Assessment

  • Thoughtful engagement in class discussions and personal reflections
  • Beginnings of brainstorming and ideation in Engineering Notebooks for the Real World Solution poster
  • Filled out responses to the feedback questions

Preparation for next class

The next lesson will be time for the students to prepare their posters. If any craft materials are available for students to use on their poster, it would be good to gather them now so that they’re available for students to use in the next lesson.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
