Introducing the Engineering and Design Process

Lesson Objectives

  • Understand the need for an engineering portfolio, and/or other forms of documentation
  • Explore methods of documentation, and the strengths and weaknesses of those methods
  • Begin to keep an Engineering Notebook

Lesson Overview

The purpose of this lesson is to introduce a separate but important aspect of Engineering, and provide the central form of assessment for more independent days of work. This is a more straightforward lesson, but it does have one activity that students may need more support with.

Materials Needed

  • 1 Notebook per Student (that will become their Engineering notebook for the Course)
  • Pencil and paper for every student
  • How to Document Video

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole class discussion on why showing your work is important. See discussion prompts to push students into contemplating this question in all aspects of their lives, and what it could mean. When finished, ask students if they think writing down their work with the EDP is important. (5 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts
    • Why is it important to show your work in math class?
    • Do you “show your work” in other classes?
    • What are some things that you could call “showing your work” even if they’re not exactly the same thing?
    • Do you think engineers have to show their work?

Monitoring Progress

  • Demonstrate the Process of Documentation with the Documentation Video (5 mins)
  • When the video is finished, have students go to their groups that built the BaseBots, turn to a fresh page in their Engineering Notebooks, and each attempt to create a multi-view drawing of their BaseBot. Have them title the page they’re drawing on “Date: First Robot” (30-35 mins)

  • Facilitation
    • If available and desired, encourage students to use colors to bring their drawings to life, through crayons, markers, or colored pencils
    • To help with the 3-D drawing, have the students first make a big hexagon, and then draw the sides and surfaces inside the hexagon, as a reference
    • Make sure to encourage students to think of this not as a test of their drawing ability, but a way to see what they can already do and what they might want to try getting better at.
      • Additionally, the real measure of the quality of the drawing isn’t how nice it looks, but how well someone else can understand it.

Wrapping Up

  • To finish the lesson, have all students write down in their Engineering Notebooks one documentation goal they have (see potential goals) and encourage them to share with the class what their goal is and why they chose it. (5-10 mins)

  • Potential Documentation Goals:
    • To make one new drawing each day
    • To fill up a whole page with one day of work
    • To put a photo in their engineering notebook
    • To make an accurate scale drawing
    • To make a whole page of just writing

Assessment

  • Successful completion of the first page of the students' engineering notebooks
  • Attempted multiview drawing

Preparation for next class

Going forward, the groups students built their BaseBots in will be referred to as their “Bot groups” for the Robot Soccer unit (we recommend making new groups later in the course for the Up and Over activity, for the purposes of variety). Additionally, their engineering notebooks will be vital tools, and are required for most lessons going forward, so students should make sure that they always have them.
