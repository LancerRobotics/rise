Developing a Manipulator

Lesson Objectives

  • Identify the constraints and criteria of designing a manipulator/intake system
  • Move towards self sufficiency in developing and iterating on designs
  • Document their thoughts and processes in their Engineering Notebooks

Lesson Overview

Using knowledge gained from past lessons, students will begin to develop their final design for the “manipulator” or intake. This lesson gives the student time to brainstorm solutions and start prototyping some potential mechanisms.

Materials Needed

  • Engineering Notebooks
  • VEX IQ Kits & Clawbots
  • Improve your Claw activity
  • Timer

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole group discussion about what are some of the potential constraints and criteria of designing a manipulator and or intake system. (5 mins)

  • Discussion prompts:
    • What are some constraints of developing the manipulator for this challenge? (e.g., size, material limits, motor availability)?
    • What are some criteria of developing the manipulator for this challenge? (e.g., grip strength, speed, reliability)?
    • What do you want this design to do? How will you achieve this?

Monitoring Progress

  • Once finished with the discussion, show students the Improve your Claw activity. They will be using this activity to test their manipulators as they go through iterations of the EDP. Dismiss students to their Bot Groups, and allow them to retrieve their materials and get to working. While they do so, set up the field to match the Improve your Claw activity. (5 mins)
  • While students are working, make sure that they are making progress and documenting their work. Documentation prompts are provided for them to use. (35 mins)

  • Documentation Prompts:
    • Using your list of constraints and criteria, what are some possible designs?
    • How are you selecting your desired design?
    • Are there any ways you can make your current design better? Are there ways to improve grip, speed, or consistency?
    • What’s one thing that isn’t working—and why?
    • What can you improve in your next iteration?

Wrapping Up

  • Have students end the class by documenting the day in their Engineering Notebooks if they haven’t already, and additionally answer the two new documentation prompts provided. (5 mins)
  • Discussion Prompts:
    • What was one challenging aspect of building a “manipulator” or intake?
    • What are some of your future plans to improve your current design?

Assessment

  • Respectful and lively discussion and collaboration
  • Documentation of every step in Engineering Notebooks
    • Adjustments made
    • Observations recorded
    • peer/team contributions

Preparation for next class

Students will be working independently for the next three lessons, so the instructor can take a backseat and allow them to pursue their own ideas and design work. That being said, the instructor will still most likely need to support by answering questions and helping students at the testing fields.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
