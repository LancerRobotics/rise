Multi-Function Driving

Lesson Objectives

  • Complete the Defend the Castle Activity
  • Adapt to growing complexity in their driving controls and develop personal styles and practices to compensate

Lesson Overview

The purpose of this lesson is for students to learn how to control more complex functions on one Robot. This also gives the students a chance to have fun with their Robots before they dive into challenging design work.

Materials Needed

  • Defend the Castle Activity
  • ClawBots and controllers
  • VEX IQ Field Materials
  • Students’ Engineering Notebooks

Getting Started

  • Begin by showing the class the Defend the Castle Activity, and then ask the class how quickly they think they could complete that challenge. Record one of their proposed times somewhere where the whole class can see, as a goal for them to reach. (5 mins)

Monitoring Progress

Once the “class goal” is recorded, have the students split into their Bot Groups and retrieve their ClawBots and controllers. While they do so, set up the activity field(s). (5 mins)


Once everything is set up, have the students complete the activity, helping to reset the field(s) when needed and supervising. Whenever a student beats the class goal time, have them write it down to become the new class goal time. As they work, encourage them to reflect on what they’re doing, and think about ways they can change their strategy. If desired, put the first 3 documentation prompts under the class goal for students to think about. (35 mins)

  • Running the Activity:
    • Make sure students don’t take more than one turn before the rest of their Bot Group members have gone
      • Also encourage Bot Groups to rotate, allowing students to all have equal time waiting for their turn
    • If possible, have multiple fields with the challenge setup running at the same time, so students don’t have to wait as long for their turn
      • You can also run multiple robots on one field to further quicken the process

Wrapping Up

  • Once all the students have finished the activity, have them open their Engineering Notebooks and write on a fresh page about what they learned about driving their Robot today. (5 mins)
  • Documentation Prompts:
    • What part of the robot do I find the easiest to control?
    • What part of the robot do I find the hardest to control?
    • What was my strategy for going the fastest?
    • Was driving easier than I thought it would be? Harder?
    • What could I have done differently that I think would have been better?

Assessment

  • Successful completion of activity
  • Documentation in Engineering Notebooks

Preparation for next class

Next class will be a head-to-head challenge, and may confuse students, due to it being a replica of the video they saw earlier in the week. It is critical to stress that this challenge and the final challenge are not the same, and this challenge is for the purposes of sparking the Engineering Design Process.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
