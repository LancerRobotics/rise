Refining Operator Input

Lesson Objectives

  • Complete the Navigate the Maze with Driver Control activity
  • Understand how operator skill contributes to the success of a robot as much as the design itself does

Lesson Overview

The purpose of this activity is for students to have a little fun and try designing something that is entirely inconsequential. It mostly serves as a little break to the non-stop learning they’ve been doing, and gives them a chance to just have fun with their resources. In addition, the students will be able to get a bit of driver practice and develop collaboration skills.

Materials Needed

  • Navigate the Maze with Driver Control activity
  • ClawBots and controllers (using the built ClawBot, not the Basebot as directed by the activity)
  • VEX field materials

Getting Started

  • Begin by having students assemble into their new Bot Groups and distributing the Clawbots and Controllers to each group. Set up the initial maze showing in the Navigate the Maze with Driver Control activity. (5 mins)

Monitoring Progress

  • After the field is set up, allow students to take turns driving through the maze. After every student has had a chance to drive their respective robot through the maze, tell students that they will have to do it again - but this time, with their eyes closed. (20 mins)
  • To do the “blind drive”, place students’ robots somewhere randomly on one side of the maze after the driver closes their eyes. Instruct the other students in the driver’s Bot Group that they will have to guide them through the maze, but can’t touch the driver or controller. Have as many students take turns driving from among the bot groups as possible. (20 mins)

Wrapping Up

  • After the “blind drive”, come back together and finish the lesson with a class discussion about their experiences and communication skills. (5 mins)
  • Discussion Prompts:
    • How did it feel to rely totally on your teammates to do something?
    • Do you feel you communicated well when helping the blind driver? Did you communicate well as a team?
    • How comfortable do you feel driving the robot?
    • Has your driving has improved with practice? Why do you think that happened?

Assessment

  • Successful completion of activity

Preparation for next class

Next class will be another single-day robot driving activity, based around adding more controls to operate at one time. Students will most likely struggle at first, but this lesson has given them a good foundation to work it out. Facilitating the next lesson will be much the same as this one, so feel free to take any learning moments from this one and apply them in the next.
