Making More Real World Connections

Lesson Objectives

  • Research robotics in the real world
  • Prepare to debate classmates respectfully

Lesson Overview

  • Students take a break from hands-on robotics to explore real-world robotics applications.
  • Each group researches one category of robotics and prepares arguments for a debate about which type is most important.

Materials Needed

  • Computers (if available)
  • Engineering Notebooks

Getting Started

  • Start with a whole-class discussion:
    • How do you think robotics is used in real life?
    • What types of robots can you think of?
    • Which type of robotics seems most important?
    • Where do you see the Engineering Design Process in everyday life?

Monitoring Progress

  • Explain that students will research robotics categories and prepare debate arguments.
  • Assign groups one of the following robotics categories:
    • Robot Dogs
    • Humanoid Robots
    • Medical Robots
    • Space Robots
    • Autonomous Drones
    • Robotic Manufacturing
    • Agricultural Robots
    • Aquatic Robots
  • Students research and take notes in Engineering Notebooks while forming arguments about why their category is the “best.”

Tips for Effective Research

  • Start broad, then refine into an argument.
  • Divide roles or collaborate together.
  • Read articles aloud so all group members understand.

Research Prompts

  • Why is this type of robotics important?
  • What can this type do that others cannot?
  • What specific part of this type of robotics matters most?
  • How does this support your argument?

Wrapping Up

  • Students share one interesting fact they learned.
  • Collect notebooks if needed for next class.
  • Discussion prompts:
    • What was the most interesting thing you learned?
    • Why did it interest you?
    • Did this research inspire you?
    • Do you still have questions?

Assessment

  • Active discussion and collaboration
  • Detailed engineering notebook research

Preparation for next class

Next class is the debate. Students must have their research ready. If more time is needed, extend research instead of rushing the debate.
