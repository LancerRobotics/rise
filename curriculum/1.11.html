Developing Designs

Lesson Objectives

  • Begin to apply the Engineering Design Process to an open-ended project
  • Repeat cycles of the Engineering Design Process in order to fully iterate on a design
  • Gain a sense of confidence through independent application of the skills they have learned thus far

Lesson Overview

This lesson begins the first independent design challenge of the course. As such, students will most likely be very eager to get started, but also probably require some support. They’ll have time to get their feet under themselves-the most important thing is that they don’t get discouraged.

Materials Needed

  • Students' Engineering Notebooks
  • Computers/projector
  • VEX IQ Field Materials
  • VEX IQ kits and Clawbots
  • Lesson 2 Practice Video
  • Manipulators in Motion Activity

Getting Started

  • Begin by having students retrieve their ClawBots and splitting into their Bot Groups. Distribute the VEX kits, and when all groups have their materials, play the Lesson 2 Practice Video for the class. (5 mins)
  • After the video, tell students to start making some sections in their Engineering Notebooks while the field is assembled. Once it is, they can test their simple Clawbots. (5-10 mins)

  • Engineering Notebook Guide
    • Each section should be a part of the Engineering Design Process
      • Those parts are: Ask, Research, Imagine, Plan, Create, Test, and Improve
    • Make sure students test their simple clawbots before moving on into making new designs
      • Any students who are waiting to test can think about other things to put in their Ask and Research sections while they wait
    • If desired, give students the EDP Graphic Organizer to help guide their work
    • The Definition documents from last lesson are included in the Resources section as well.

Monitoring Progress

  • Once the field is set up, students can begin testing their ClawBots. Encourage students to test quickly and then go back to work with their Bot Group in their Engineering Notebooks. Make sure students clean up the field after they test, for the next group (2 mins)
  • After they test, encourage students to then follow the EDP to develop their designs and ideas. It will provide a natural progression for them to follow. (30 mins)
  • As students are working, circulate around the classroom and look and listen for student engagement. Encourage students to “think out loud”, and ask them their ideas and thought processes. Answer any questions students might have, and offer help, but try to let them sort out problems on their own. Some questions might be helpful for the entire class, at which point, you can pause the class and have the student repeat the question for you to answer in front of the whole class.

  • Testing Notes:
    • Students should record test results in the "Research" section of their Notebooks, as well as their ideas and thinking after the test-such as
      • What went well
      • What didn't go well
      • What are some conclusions that can be drawn based off the testing-for example
        • ___ needs to be improved
        • ___ struggled to do ___
        • ___ does ___ well

Wrapping Up

  • Have students save their work in the saving area designated for their Robots, and clean up any materials that aren’t part of an active construction. Have students briefly reflect on what they would like to accomplish tomorrow and what went well today in their Engineering Notebooks. (5 mins)

  • Documentation Prompts
    • What went well about my work today?
    • Did I follow the steps of the EDP? Why or why not?
      • If not, would it be better if I changed my approach?
    • What do I want to accomplish tomorrow?

    • Assessment

    • Documentation of every step in the Engineering Notebook
    • Lively conversation as students complete iterations

    Preparation for next class

    Next class will be a continuation of this one, so keeping the field setup will save time at the beginning of class, if possible - if not, it isn’t an issue. Next class, try being as hands off as possible, and let the students’ prior knowledge and creativity guide them as far as it can.
