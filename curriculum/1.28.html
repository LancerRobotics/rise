Reaching for the Sky

Lesson Objectives

  • Identify the constraints and criteria of designing a lift arm system
  • Look at advantages and disadvantages of different arm designs
  • Move towards self sufficiency in developing and iterating on designs
  • Document their thoughts and processes in their Engineering Notebooks

Lesson Overview

The purpose of this activity is to introduce students to different arm designs. Students will optimize their arms just like they did for their manipulators to better complete their objective. They will document their progress and iterate through designs to find the best ones.

Materials Needed

  • VEX IQ Kits
  • Improve Your Arm Practice Activity
  • IQ - Arm Design Lesson Summary
  • Engineering Notebook

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole group discussion on the importance of an arm regarding an intake system. Show the types of arms as seen in the IQ - Arm Design Lesson Summary, and explain each arm’s strengths and weaknesses as shown in the document. (10 mins)
  • After the discussion and demonstration, allow students to disperse into their Bot Groups to retrieve their materials and begin to work, and while they do set up the field as shown in the Improve Your Arm Practice Activity. (35 mins)

  • Discussion prompts:
    • How are robot arms different from human arms?
    • How is an arm important to an intake system?
    • What do you need to think about when creating an arm for a robot?
    • What are some strengths and weaknesses you can think of for different arms?

Monitoring Progress

  • As students are working, circulate around the classroom and look for signs of experimentation and documentation. Make sure that each iteration the students do is properly documented in their engineering notebooks.

  • Facilitation Questions:
    • Which arms have the best tradeoffs for advantages and disadvantages?
    • How complicated is building an arm?

Wrapping Up

  • Come back together for a whole group discussion about their progress. Ask them about what type of arm they think is the best. Ask them about their documenting progress and how it will help them.
  • Discussion Prompts:
    • How do you think the iteration process will help you with experience with the claw?
    • What type of arms seem to work the best for your objectives?

Assessment

  • Successful progress of activity
  • Evidence of documenting

Preparation for next class

In the next lesson, students will continue to work on their projects. Make sure their bots are stored in a safe place and that their devices are charged to be used again next class. If applicable, ensure their engineering notebooks are stored in a dry area when not in use.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
