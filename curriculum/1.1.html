What is Robotics?

Lesson Objectives

  • Define what a robot is and explain what makes them up
  • Identify Real-World Applications of Robots
  • Define what an Engineer is and what they do

Lesson Overview

The purpose is to introduce students to the idea of robotics and engineering, and have them start focusing attention towards different robotics ideas. In addition, students will gain foundational knowledge about the usage of robotics in everyday life. Students will break off into pairs and draw their own ideas of what their ideal robot would do.

Materials Needed

  • Paper and Pencil for every student
  • Rise Introduction Video

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole-group discussion about robots. Ask the students about what they think robots are, and if they’ve seen robots being used in their everyday lives. If they haven’t, ask them about what they think a robot is and what tasks in their lives would be easier if they had one. (10-15 mins)
  • Show the RISE introduction video. (4 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts:
    • What do you think a robot is?
    • Robots can do a lot of things by themselves. How would you use a robot to help in your everyday life?
    • What parts do you think you need to make a robot?

Monitoring Progress

  • Break off into pairs, distribute paper and pencils, and ask groups to draw their own ideal robot. (20 mins)
  • As students are working, walk around to different groups and look and listen for student engagement. Encourage students to talk to their partners and come up with multiple ideas.

  • Facilitation Questions:
    • What parts of your robot help it solve the problem you want it to solve?
    • Have you seen or heard of anything in real life that could do what your robot can do?
    • What would you like to learn about robots in our lessons?

Wrapping Up

  • Come back together for a whole group discussion about their drawings. Ask for volunteers to show everybody their drawings and tell the group what their ideal robot would do. Have them point out parts on their robot that would help it complete their tasks. (10 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts:
    • How easy do you think it is to make a robot?
    • What issues do you think you would run into if you tried to create one right now?
    • How would the robots that you drew help your community?

Assessment

  • Students can describe what makes something a robot
  • Students can name at least three examples of robotics in everyday life

Preparation for next class

Next class will focus on getting into the actual kit. Be prepared to have open and flat areas (ideally tables), as the students will need to take out parts from the kit and have room to put them. Some parts are small, so make sure to be prepared to tell the students to be careful not to lose anything.
