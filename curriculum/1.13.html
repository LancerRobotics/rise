The Importance of Field Testing

Lesson Objectives

  • Compete in the One on One Robot Soccer Challenge
  • Learn the importance of pushing a design to its limits in order to gather data
  • Understand the necessity for multiple rounds of various forms of testing in order to ensure the success of a design

Lesson Overview

This lesson supplements the previous two days by introducing another aspect of design testing that is important for engineering. The workflow will continue as it has for the past two lessons, once the additional concept is introduced.

Materials Needed

  • VEX IQ Robotics Kit components)
  • VEX IQ kits and ClawBots
  • VEX IQ field elements
  • One on One Robot Soccer Challenge
  • Field Testing Video

Getting Started

  • Begin with a whole group discussion about whether or not failure can be a good thing. Introduce the idea of limits, or how far something can go before it breaks. (5 mins)
  • Once the discussion is concluded, show the class the Field Testing Video, followed by an explanation of the One on One Robot Soccer Challenge. (5 mins & 5 mins)
  • After the video, allow groups to break off into their Bot Groups and retrieve their kits, ClawBots, and any of their detached work. Students will complete rounds of testing and design as they have been previously for the rest of the lesson. (30 mins)

  • Discussion Prompts
    • Can failure be a good thing?
      • Is there only one kind of failure? Or are there multiple types?
    • When you “push yourself to the limit” what are you doing?
      • What is a limit?
    • Do you think it could ever be good to push something past its limit?

Monitoring Progress

  • Make sure to inform the students that the One on One Robot Soccer Challenge is meant purely for testing purposes, and scores don’t count for anything other than data. Because testing is now occurring with this challenge and not the Manipulators in Motion activity, students will need a partner Bot Group to properly test. If a group is in desperate need of a partner, but no peers are available, the instructor can use one hand to mimic the general actions a Robot would be taking in the challenge.

  • Facilitation
    • Encourage students to learn from what goes wrong, rather than be defeated by it.
      • A handy phrase is “Failure is expected, but giving up is not!”
    • The same ideas of facilitation are present in this day as they were in the past two
    • Emphasize that scores don’t count right now

Wrapping Up

  • Have students save their work in the saving area designated for their Robots, and clean up any materials that aren’t part of an active construction. Have students briefly reflect on what they would like to accomplish tomorrow, and what went well today. (5 mins)

  • Documentation Prompts:
    • What went well about my work today?
    • Is my strategy working well with my manipulator design? Why or why not?
    • What do I want to accomplish tomorrow?

Assessment

  • Documentation of every step in the Engineering Notebook
  • Friendly competition in the One on One robot soccer challenge
  • Lively conversation as students complete iterations

Preparation for next class

Work will continue with students in their Bot Groups iterating on their designs, so again, saving the field is better but not a requirement. Next lesson will be back to the more independent structure of the lesson before this, so the same instructor resources and facilitation ideas will apply.
