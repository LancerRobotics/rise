Getting Started: Robot Soccer

Lesson Objectives

  • Complete assembly of the basic clawbot
  • Understand the rules and objectives of the Robot Soccer Challenge

Lesson Overview

This lesson is very simple, simply involving students watching a tutorial video for most of class. Because it is their first real robot, they will be operating off the same common template in order to then develop their design further afterward. It is also important for them to understand the game they will be competing in, so be sure not to move on until all students are ready.

Materials Needed

  • VEX IQ Robotics Kit components
  • Robot Soccer game introduction video
  • BaseBot instruction video (The third video down on the page)

Getting Started

  • Begin by sorting the class again into groups to distribute IQ kits (Note: they will be working in these groups for the next few weeks, so ensure that each group will get along well over this period of time!)
  • Once distributed, ask them if they’re excited to build their first robots. Make sure they don’t open the kits yet.
  • Once groups are settled, show them the Robot Soccer video, which will show them the game. Explain the rules, as in the reference. Open the floor to any questions they have, to ensure everyone understands fully.
  • Robot Soccer Rules Reference:
    • Each match is 60 seconds
      • The timer is paused when resetting the field after a goal
    • Each team, red and blue, consists of two robots
    • The base robot all groups will build, but afterwards they will be modified as the students desire
    • The Field is 6 tiles by four tiles, with a two-tile gap in the wall as a “goal” on either end
    • Each team wants to grab the cube in the center and push it all of the way outside the field, through the opposing side’s goal
      • Color of the cube doesn’t matter, but none of it can be inside the field, and it must go through the goals to count

Monitoring Progress

  • Once finished with introducing the game, have the students open their kits, and put the BaseBot instruction video in a place where it’s easily visible to the whole class. The rest of the lesson will be them following this video.
  • While they watch the video, make sure to make yourself available in case they have any questions or need any help.

  • Facilitation
    • Make sure each group is keeping pace with the video, and if not, help them by pausing or rewinding as needed.
      • This may frustrate some groups who move quicker, but remind them that they’re all part of a class and that for everyone to learn, they must be patient.
    • Continue to offer your presence as help, for any questions or technical help the students may need.

Wrapping Up

  • Once all groups are finished with the BaseBot, have the groups attach the 2x8 “smooth” plate to it in a place that is easily accessible. Once they do, have them write each of their names on it with a dry erase marker. This will serve as the bot’s “nametag,” indicating which group it belongs to so it can be easily found and differentiated from other bots.
  • Congratulate the class on finishing their first robot!
  • Name Tag Note
    • Students should start with just their names, but eventually they might want a team name, or some other unique indicator. The nametags are purely for organization, so this is fine as long as the bots can be distinguished from each other.

Assessment

  • Successful completion of BaseBot

Preparation for next class

Next class will be less video-based, so while the students work in this lesson might be a good time to review any material you feel is necessary.
