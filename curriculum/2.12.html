Lesson 12: Debugging

Objective(s)

  • Introduce and learn simple debugging strategies to fix errors

Materials Needed

  • Computers (1–2 per group)
  • Basebots
  • VEXcode IQ website
  • Notebooks and writing utensils for each student
  • Pre-Made “Buggy code examples”

Lesson Procedure

1. Overview

  • Students will get a better understanding of simple debugging strategies to efficiently and effectively weed out missing/unwanted code.

2. Getting Started

  • Have the students think and write down in their notebooks the answer to the following question:
    • “Have you ever had something of yours break? What steps did you take to identify the problem and fix it?”
  • Go over this Document with the students and have them take notes and answer the questions on the document.

3. Monitoring Progress

  • Make sure that the students are answering the questions on the document accordingly.
  • After reading the document, if there is time, have students do a class-wide exercise.
    • Have the students create “bugs” within their own bot’s code, and make sure that they write the bug down so that they don’t forget what it was.
    • Have these students then switch with another group and have them try to locate and fix the bug using the strategies that were presented in the document.
    • Also, make sure that the group that is attempting to fix these errors are writing down any changes they make into a notebook, as well as the reasoning for why they added certain things into the code.

4. Wrapping Up

  • Students should take the time to reflect on the lesson and think of more ways, outside of a robotics environment where they can “debug” problems in the real world.

Assessment

  • Students should understand what “Debugging” is and some basic strategies that they can employ to isolate and tackle issues.
  • Students should start to get into the mindset of reducing larger problems into smaller and more manageable ones.

Preparation for next class

The next class will be an independent exploration day where students can test other blocks or brush up on previous blocks that they aren’t too familiar with. After that, they will use what they learned to work on a larger project.
