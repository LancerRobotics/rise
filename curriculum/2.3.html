Lesson 3: Importance of Pseudocode

Lesson Objectives

  • Learn what pseudocode is and why it is important in robotics.
  • Write and test simple pseudocode for a robot movement task.

Lesson Overview

This lesson’s purpose is to introduce students to the idea of pseudocode and the importance of organization and having an outline in programming. Students will then try to use their new knowledge to develop a solution to a challenging activity, which will be actually programmed and run in a future lesson.

Materials Needed

  • Notebooks for each student
  • Pseudocode document

Lesson Procedure

Getting Started

  • Begin with a class discussion on the following question: If you wanted a robot to clean your room, what instructions would you give it? You have to give all of the instructions to the robot first, and then the robot will follow those steps to clean the room.
    • Have students elaborate on what the robot would do first, second, and so on. Also, talk about how the robot would do it, for example, a robot must bend down to pick up clothes.

Monitoring Progress

  • Go through this document with the students, and make sure they write down important information in their notebooks. Students should just get a general idea of the definitions and why pseudocode is important, but they should pay very close attention to the example given of walking in a square.

Wrapping Up

  • Now, students will use what they learned in a similar task and make an outline for this challenge: Make the robot go in a pentagon (5 sides) of side length 200 mm. (This uses a little bit of math!)
    • Students should work with their groups to try and figure out the problem. If a group can’t figure it out, they are encouraged to ask other groups about their ideas instead of asking a teacher for the answer.
  • Afterwards, have a short class discussion and have each group share what ideas they had.

Assessment

  • Students understand what pseudocode is and why it should be done for every program
  • Students understand how to create an outline for a task and the format for a pseudocode

Preparation for next class

In the next class, we will go back to the computers and the robots, and the students will code and run the pentagon challenge today while learning about a couple of blocks and how they work.
