Competition Day

Lesson Objectives

  • Compete in the modified Up and Over Challenge
  • Enjoy a friendly competition

Lesson Overview

  • Today is the culmination of all the work and learning, and is meant to be fun above all else!
  • This is an opportunity for students to be proud of their work and see it in action. The opening discussion is meant to ensure that no student’s enjoyment comes at the expense of another’s.

Materials Needed

  • VEX IQ Robotics Kit components
  • Clawbots and controllers
  • VEX Field Pieces
  • Up and Over

Getting Started

  • Begin with a quick discussion about the competition and the purpose of it. Remind students in the discussion that the purpose of this competition is to show the students their growth in STEM and engineering.
    • It’s not about winning -> it’s about the growth the students have shown over the weeks
    • That being said, healthy competition is good and fun-but this course is about the whole class as a team, not just one Bot Group over another.

  • Discussion prompts:
    • What is your strategy for this competition?
    • How do you hope your design will do?
    • Do you remember the rules?
    • Can you make sure to have fun without putting other teams down?

Monitoring Progress

  • When students are ready, assemble the competition field (or multiple, if able). Run rounds of the competition, and keep track of the fastest time on the board if desired. Students should keep track of their times either on their own or on the board. Encourage students to spectate when not playing themselves, and to trade drivers when making more attempts at the challenge so all students get a chance to drive their robots.
  • Tips for running the challenge
    • To get as many matches in as possible, create a match schedule so teams know when they will be playing next.
    • Set up designated areas where teams can watch matches.
    • The matches should be fast enough that all groups can have multiple chances to attempt it. This is intended, but make sure all groups are getting equal opportunity.

Wrapping Up

  • When finished, put away all materials and robots, and then come back together for a group discussion. Ask the students what they thought of the challenge and how they did. Ask students if they were satisfied with their robot’s performance.
  • Discussion Prompts:
    • Did your robot perform as well as you thought it would be able to?
    • Knowing now, how would you change your robot to improve its performance?

Assessment

  • Successful completion of activity

Preparation for next class

Next class will be about connecting again to real world careers and fields, so the robots will not be required. Keep everything in a safe place until deconstruction, which will come in a later lesson.

Resources

  • Poster Guidelines
    • The Real World Solution poster should consist of, at least:
      • The problem being solved
      • One drawing of the imagined solution
      • Three briefly described details of the solution
      • The method by which the solution solves the problem
      • What type or types of engineering the solution falls under
      • Why the solution falls under that type (or types)
    • This is a minimum, so creativity is highly encouraged and students are free to go over this minimum as much as they like, as long as all the requirements are met
    • Most of all, the poster should be fun to both make and look at.
    • When presenting, all students involved in creating should speak for a fairly even amount of time with the others, but students can split up the subjects in whatever way they please.
